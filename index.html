02 / Research & collaboration

M.S. Software Engineering thesisPublished / 2026

Attention-Aware
VR Classroom

Effectiveness of Quiz-Based Interventions in Virtual Reality Lectures on Learning

I designed and implemented an eye-tracked VR classroom to investigate when quizzes should interrupt an immersive lecture. The system compares no-intervention, fixed-timer, and gaze-based attention conditions.

My work Lecture orchestration, gaze and region-of-interest interpretation, an interpretable attention model, head-dwell quiz input, and structured session exports for research analysis. Developed at Kennesaw State University with thesis advisor Dr. Sungchul Jung.

A 29-participant study compared the three conditions. It did not detect a statistically significant difference in learning outcomes.

Human–computer interaction studyIEEE VR / 2026

Exoskeleton
Chair in VR

Use of Exoskeleton Chair for Supporting Posture Change in VR Experience

A posture-responsive VR scooter task comparing conventional sitting, unsupported standing, and exoskeleton-supported movement. The study examined fatigue, cybersickness, experience, and task performance.

My role First author and VR research developer, working with Dr. Sungchul Jung at Kennesaw State University. The application uses posture calibration and a scooter-and-coin task to explore physical support during VR interaction.

Exoskeleton support reduced fatigue relative to unsupported standing, while task performance and experience varied by condition.

04 / Approach

Curiosity starts it.

My work now spans immersive research, independent software, multiplayer games, and applied systems engineering. I enjoy collaborating with people who bring a different perspective, then working through the details together.

I use Codex as a development partner for implementation, debugging, documentation, and review. My own hands-on engine work, creative direction, integration decisions, playtesting, and real-hardware testing drive the result.
